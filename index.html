How it works

Three moves. Zero tools.

Sit it down

Stand your guitar or bass upright in the seat, body on the cushion, neck against the headrest.

Hook one side

Clip the first hook onto a metal headrest post, then run the soft strap across the neck.

Hook the other

Clip the second hook onto the other post. The neck is held, the guitar stays put, you drive.

That's the whole product.

Two plastic hooks and a soft strap. Nothing to break, nothing to assemble, and small enough to live in your glove box or center console.

Origin story

It started at the checkout counter.

About three years ago I bought my very first guitar: the cheapest acoustic at Guitar Center, $150. At checkout the clerk asked if I wanted a case, so I went to look. Even the cheapest one was around $60.

I asked him what a case was actually for. Dents, scratches, and dust, he said. Dust I can wipe off. And dents and scratches from what? "From transporting it. Taking it to a friend's house, or to lessons."

Forty percent of the guitar, to stop theoretical scratches. The math wasn't even close.

Most beginner guitars never go anywhere. They sit at home, and a lot of them end up in the closet within a year, right next to the case that cost $60.

So the clerk told me what everyone else does: put a towel down in the trunk and lay the guitar on it. That's what I did on the way to lessons. It kind of works. It also slides around on every turn, and I'd be listening to it thump back there when I should have been paying attention to the road.

The Guitar Seatbelt is the fix I wanted that day. The guitar rides up front where I can see it, it doesn't move, and it costs less than a quarter of the cheapest case.

FAQ

Fair questions.

Does it work with a bass?

Yes. It's designed for acoustic guitars, electric guitars, and basses. The strap goes around the neck, so body shape doesn't matter.

Will it fit my car?

It hooks onto the two metal posts under an adjustable headrest, which most cars have, front seats and back. It won't work on seats with a one-piece, built-in headrest.

Will the strap hurt my strings or neck?

The strap is soft and only rests across the neck to keep it from tipping or sliding. It isn't cinched down hard.

Is it a crash-safety device?

No. It keeps your instrument from sliding and tipping during normal driving. It is not a crash-tested restraint, and it doesn't replace a case for serious protection.
